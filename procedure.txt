How To Think About LLD

Think of LLD as:

"How would I model and organize this backend system cleanly?"

NOT:

Perfect UML

Every design pattern

Enterprise architecture diagrams

Your goal:

Clean entities

Clear relationships

Extensibility

Workflows

Concurrency awareness

LLD Interview Framework

Use this exact flow for every question.

1. Clarify Requirements

Ask:

Core features?

Scale/concurrency concerns?

Single machine or distributed?

What's out of scope?

2. Identify Core Entities

Ask:

"What are the nouns in this system?"

Examples:

Uber:

Rider

Driver

Trip

Vehicle

Booking:

User

Show

Seat

Reservation

Scheduler:

Job

Worker

Queue

3. Define Relationships

Examples:

Driver HAS-A Vehicle

Trip HAS-A Rider

Reservation BELONGS TO User

Keep it simple.

4. Add Interfaces / Strategies

Ask:

"What business logic may change later?"

Examples:

PricingStrategy

MatchingStrategy

RetryStrategy

Filter

This is often the strongest SDE-2 signal.

5. Define Main Workflow

Explain:

How requests enter

How processing happens

State transitions

Failure paths

6. Discuss Concurrency

Always mention:

Race conditions

Locks

Atomic updates

Thread safety

Common examples:

Double seat booking

Two drivers accepting same ride

Multiple workers processing same job

7. Discuss Extensibility

Ask:

"What feature might product ask for next?"

Examples:

New pricing algorithm

New retry policy

New notification channel

LLD Interview Advice

Start with requirements. A wrong assumption can derail the entire design.

Spend most of your time on entities, relationships, and workflows. That's where most interview value lies.

Don't force design patterns. Use them only when they solve a real extensibility problem.

Always discuss concurrency if shared resources exist (seats, jobs, rides, inventory, lockers, etc.).

Think about what business logic might change in the future and abstract only those parts.

Keep the design simple. Interviewers generally prefer a clean design with sensible tradeoffs over an overengineered one.

Communicate your thought process continuously instead of silently drawing diagrams.

If stuck, fall back to the framework: Requirements → Entities → Relationships → Strategies → Workflow → Concurrency → Extensibility.

Focus on demonstrating structured thinking, not perfect UML or every design pattern you've learned.

Remember: Most candidates fail LLD interviews due to lack of structure, not lack of knowledge. A clear, organized approach often matters more than the final design itself.
